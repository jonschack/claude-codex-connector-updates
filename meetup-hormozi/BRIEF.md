# Meetup Marketing Brief — Cracked Claude Cowork & Codex Club

This is the single source of truth the `meetup-marketer` subagent reads before applying the
Hormozi frameworks. Update this file as the meetup evolves; everything downstream keys off it.

## The "product" (what we're marketing)
A **free, in-person Phoenix meetup**: a ~1-hour **live capability deep-dive** showcasing the most
mind-blowing recent Connector/MCP capabilities — run as live demos on real tools — so attendees
learn what each piece of software can *actually do* and **leave knowing the exact prompts to use**.
Voice is **"show don't hype"**: we don't claim it's mind-blowing, we demo it live and let the room
decide (and we show where it breaks). Audience is a **broad/mixed room** — builders, operators,
founders, creators, and the AI-curious; no coding required to attend.

> Format note: this evolved from the earlier "bring-a-workflow Q&A" framing into a
> curated capability deep-dive (decided 2026-06-01). Open Q&A can still bookend the demos.

> IMPORTANT FRAMING: This is a FREE event. There is no price. So apply Hormozi's **value
> equation** to the *attendance decision*, not to a dollar amount. The "cost" an attendee pays is
> **their time (~1 hour), the friction of showing up in person, and their skepticism/AI-fatigue.**
> Maximize: (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time + Effort + Skepticism). Do NOT invent
> ticket prices, payment tiers, or money-back guarantees. "Risk reversal" = de-risking their time
> (e.g., "leave with at least one usable answer or we failed").

## Who runs it
**automationinterns.com** — engineering grads and top students *forward-deployed inside local
Phoenix businesses*, automating the processes eating owners' time. Credibility = hands-on, in the
trenches with real local businesses, not theory. We run these because the questions worth asking
are too specific for a YouTube video.

## Dream outcomes (what attendees actually want)
- See what's genuinely possible now and feel *caught up* — not drowning in AI news.
- Walk away knowing the **exact prompts** to trigger capabilities they didn't know existed.
- A straight hype-vs-real filter from people who use these tools daily (what to use, what to skip).
- Make a confident next move instead of guessing.

## Audience / avatars (broad/mixed room)
1. **The Hands-On Builder/Power User** — founder, freelancer, dev, or automation-curious operator
   who already drives AI tools and wants the cutting edge of what connectors/MCP can do.
2. **The Overwhelmed Operator** — owner/operator who hears endless AI hype and wants to see what's
   real and what to actually ask, without needing to be technical.
3. **The Skeptical Doer** — heard about tools but doesn't trust them; persuaded by live proof, not
   claims. (Show-don't-hype is aimed squarely at them.)

## The experience / "value stack" (what they get for their hour)
- Live demos of jaw-dropping-but-real capabilities (trade, voice-call, edit-video-by-transcript,
  in-chat dashboards, browser control, precise computation), each with the prompt that triggers it.
- A fast breadth tour across the directory's categories so everyone sees something for their work.
- An honest hype-vs-real filter + safety basics (human-in-the-loop where it touches money/systems).
- Take-home **"What to Ask Your AI" cheat-sheet** built from what gets demoed (doubles as the
  reason-to-attend and a shareable recruiting asset).

## Core objections to neutralize
- "I'm too busy / don't have an hour." · "AI is overhyped — this'll be more breathless demos."
- "I'm not technical / this'll go over my head." · "Why a meetup and not a YouTube video?"
- "Will any of this be relevant to me?" (answer: breadth tour + you leave with usable prompts.)

## Constraints & voice
- Phoenix-area, in-person. Free RSVP (Meetup.com).
- Voice: confident, practical, local, **no-hype/"show don't hype"**, builder energy. "Cracked" =
  highly skilled, not jargon-y. Broad/mixed room — speak to builders AND non-technical operators.
- Credibility guardrail: every capability is demoed live or explicitly labeled a vendor claim;
  trading/financial demos stay human-in-the-loop. Never overclaim — the whole positioning rests on it.
- Goal metric: **RSVPs + actual attendance** for the first event.
