# 03 — Objection Destroyer

> For each objection: the surface form, the hidden belief underneath, the belief shift, a one-line
> reframe for copy, and the proof that supports it. Prioritized by impact on RSVPs.

---

## Priority Order (highest RSVP impact first)

1. "I don't have time / too busy"
2. "AI is overhyped / not for a business like mine"
3. "Will this actually be specific to MY situation, or just generic?"
4. "I'm not technical"
5. "Why a meetup? Why not just watch a YouTube video / Google it?"

---

## Objection 1 — "I don't have time / I'm too busy"

**Surface form:** "I want to come but I just don't have an hour right now."

**Hidden belief:** Attending is a cost, not an investment. The payoff is uncertain. An hour of
potential value is not worth an hour of definite busyness.

**Category:** Time / Risk

**Belief shift:** One focused hour at this event saves more time than it costs — because you'll
stop spinning on AI questions you currently can't answer and start doing something specific.

**Proof:** The format is live Q&A + brainstorm — not a lecture. You bring one real question, it
gets addressed, you leave with an answer. This isn't background learning; it's a direct exchange.
The people running it work in Phoenix businesses daily, so answers are immediately applicable, not
theoretical.

**One-line reframe for copy:**
> "One hour to stop wasting time wondering and start knowing what to do."

**Where to use:** Event page problem section, social posts, DM responses.

---

## Objection 2 — "AI is overhyped / not for a business like mine"

**Surface form:** "I've heard this before. It's not really relevant to what I do."

**Hidden belief:** AI tools are built for tech companies or enterprises. My business is different —
smaller, more specific, more hands-on. The hype doesn't apply here.

**Category:** Identity / Trust

**Belief shift:** The skepticism is valid. A lot of AI hype is irrelevant to small operators.
That's exactly why this event exists — to cut through it and focus only on what's real for local
Phoenix businesses.

**Proof:** automationinterns.com is embedded in local businesses — not SaaS startups, not
enterprise IT. The answers come from practitioners who see what actually works (and doesn't) at
the scale and type of businesses in the room. Skepticism is welcome. It makes the conversation
better.

**One-line reframe for copy:**
> "Skeptical? Good. That's the right starting point. Bring that and a real workflow."

**Where to use:** Event title subtext, event page hero, social posts targeting Skeptical Doer,
FAQ section.

---

## Objection 3 — "Will this actually be specific to MY situation, or just generic?"

**Surface form:** "Every event like this ends up being too broad to be useful."

**Hidden belief:** The format will default to a general AI overview. My specific workflow, my
specific tool question, my specific business won't actually get addressed.

**Category:** Trust / Value

**Belief shift:** The format is built around your specific situation, not a pre-built curriculum.
You bring the workflow or question. The room works on it. There's no slide deck covering "Top 5
AI Trends." There's a live conversation about what you brought in.

**Proof:** Interactive brainstorm format — not a presentation. The organizers are from
automationinterns.com, which runs this model with actual businesses (not in classrooms). The
take-home cheat-sheet is prompted by what comes up in the room, not pre-written generically.

**One-line reframe for copy:**
> "Bring your actual workflow. This isn't a survey of AI — it's a working session on your problem."

**Where to use:** Event page solution/mechanism section, FAQ, social posts addressing the
"generic event" fatigue.

---

## Objection 4 — "I'm not technical"

**Surface form:** "I don't have a tech background. This probably isn't for me."

**Hidden belief:** AI tools require a technical skill set I don't have. I'll be lost or
embarrassed. The conversation will go over my head.

**Category:** Identity / Effort

**Belief shift:** The attendees are operators, not engineers. The goal isn't to learn to code —
it's to figure out what to ask your tools and what to hand off. The event is run by engineering
grads who've learned to translate technical concepts for business owners. Non-technical is the
default audience, not the exception.

**Proof:** The take-home asset is literally titled "what to ask your AI" — it's built for non-
technical people to direct AI tools without needing to understand how they work. The conversations
at previous automationinterns.com engagements are with operators, not developers.

**One-line reframe for copy:**
> "You don't need a technical background. You need a workflow problem. We'll handle the rest."

**Where to use:** Event page FAQ, social targeting operators explicitly, flyer subtext.

---

## Objection 5 — "Why a meetup? Why not just Google it / watch YouTube?"

**Surface form:** "I can find this information online without committing to showing up somewhere."

**Hidden belief:** My questions are general enough that existing resources can answer them.
The in-person format doesn't add enough over what I can get asynchronously.

**Category:** Effort / Value

**Belief shift:** Generic resources give generic answers. Your specific workflow, your specific
tool question, your specific business context — those don't exist in a YouTube video. The format
delivers crowd-sourced, real-time answers that respond to your exact situation. That's not
available at any search query.

**Proof:** automationinterns.com runs because their clients' questions are too specific for
online resources — that's the literal origin of the format. The event description says: "The
questions worth asking are too specific for a YouTube video. That's why we do this in person."

**One-line reframe for copy:**
> "The questions worth asking are too specific for a YouTube video. That's why we do this in person."

**Where to use:** Event page, social copy, flyer tagline, DM responses.
