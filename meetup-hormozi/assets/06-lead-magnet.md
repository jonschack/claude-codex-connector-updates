### Section 1: The Rule
**Better prompts = better answers. Bad prompts = generic garbage.**

The single most common mistake: asking your AI what it would do instead of telling it your
specific situation and asking what *you* should do.

**Weak:** "How do I follow up with leads?"
**Strong:** "I sell [service] to [customer type] in Phoenix. My average lead goes cold after
[X days]. Write me a 3-message follow-up sequence that doesn't sound like a robot."

---

### Section 2: The Prompt Framework (use this for any task)
```
CONTEXT: I run a [type of business] in Phoenix. [Brief description of the situation].
TASK: [What you want the AI to do — be specific].
CONSTRAINTS: [What it shouldn't do, or limits to work within].
FORMAT: [How you want the output — bullet list, email, table, step-by-step].
```

*You don't need to follow this exactly — just make sure your prompt has context, a clear task,
and a format. Most prompts fail because they're missing at least one of these.*

---

### Section 3: The 6 Prompts Worth Knowing

**1. Draft a customer message you've been putting off**
> "I need to send a message to a [client/vendor/customer] about [situation]. I want it to be
> [professional / direct / friendly]. Here's the context: [paste what happened]. Write the message."

**2. Summarize a long document or email chain**
> "Here's a [document / email thread / contract section]. Summarize the key points in 3–5 bullets.
> Flag anything that needs my attention."

**3. Build a simple checklist for a recurring process**
> "I have a process I do every [week/month] for [task]. Walk me through building a checklist so
> someone else could do it. Ask me questions if you need more detail."

**4. Write a job post or SOW for a role you need to fill**
> "I need to hire someone to [role/task] for my business. Help me write a job post. Here's what
> the person will actually do: [your description]. Keep it under 300 words."

**5. Find the bottleneck in a workflow you explained**
> "Here's how we currently handle [process]: [your description]. Where do you think we're losing
> the most time or creating the most errors? What would you look at first?"

**6. Turn a brain dump into a structured plan**
> "Here's a mess of notes from a meeting / things I need to figure out: [paste notes]. Organize
> this into a clear action list with owners and deadlines."

---

### Section 4: The One Rule for Automation
**Automate a process you understand, not one you're still figuring out.**

If you can describe a process in a step-by-step checklist, it can probably be automated.
If you're still improvising it each time, automate the checklist first.

Most automation failures happen because the process wasn't defined before the tool was bought.
